What it is

The Sincereware Receipt.

A receipt is the deal, on one screen. Before you sign up or pay, it answers the six questions people have, in plain words anyone can read in thirty seconds.

It sits beside the privacy policy and terms: they hold the legal detail, the receipt is the honest summary, and the two must never disagree. Every line has to be checkable in the product itself. If a question can't be answered plainly, the product still has a decision to make.

The standard

The eight principles.

i

Useful and restrained

Do one clear thing well. Complexity added to create dependency is a defect.

ii

Users are not inventory

No advertising or surveillance as the business model.

iii

Honest payment

Charging is fine when it funds real value or real cost. The objection is to extraction.

iv

No unjustified subscriptions

Recurring payment needs recurring value or cost. What you bought once stays yours.

v

Agency, including the exit

Clear pricing, accessible data, and a real way to leave.

vi

No behavioural coercion

Mechanics may motivate in the moment. They never punish absence, manufacture urgency, or charge to relieve pressure the product created.

vii

Growth shared with users

Where a product runs on recurring support, scale should lower what supporters pay.

viii

Inspectable claims

Every commitment must be checkable in the product itself, and so must what the product tells you is true. That is what the receipt is for.

Tools and play

Tools and games alike: a record of what you did is fine, a penalty for stepping away is not, and relief is never for sale.

Pricing model

The Community Rate.

A feature costs money to run and improve. As more people support it, the price falls for everyone, including the people who joined first.

Everyone pays less as more join

Steps are published in advance. When one is reached, every subscriber drops to the new price. The price rises only with notice.

Only for real ongoing cost

New content that keeps arriving, or real running costs. What you bought once stays yours.

Boundaries

Sincereware’s limits.

  • • Not "everything must be free." Software that can't survive isn't automatically ethical.
  • • Not anti-measurement. Knowing how a product is used is how it gets better. The line is selling it, or using it against the people it came from.
  • • Not a badge or a certification. Nobody audits anybody. Take the language if it fits, and publish your own receipt.
